🛠 How to Read and Extract Ideas from These Recent Works:

    1. Start with Abstract + Introduction + Contributions
        _ Write down: What is the new idea or improvement?
        _ What problem does it address that earlier systems didn’t?
    2. Look for Architecture Diagrams
        _ They often show modules like “Retriever”, “Generator / Reader”, “Verification / Explainability”, “Fusion / Aggregation.”
        _ Compare their architecture modules with yours — note extra modules they add, or simplify.
    3.Read Method / Model Sections Closely
        _ Focus particularly on retrieval techniques, reasoning / multi-hop modules, fusion of context, visual / layout modeling, explainability.
        _ Mark parts you don’t fully understand yet; sometimes you can skip deep math but understand the mechanism in words.
    4. Evaluation / Experiments
        _ See which datasets they use (e.g. DocHop-QA).
        _ What metrics do they report? (Accuracy, F1, latency, interpretability)
        _ Compare  performance of baseline vs their method, especially in “hard” cases (multi-hop, ambiguous queries).
    5. Ablation Studies and Error Analysis
        _ See which components contribute the most.
        _ See failure cases: what do they struggle with?
        _ That gives you ideas for what your system might struggle with and what to plan for in future.
    6.Limitations / Future Work
        _ Authors often admit what they couldn’t solve — use those as “inspiration” or “caution” for planning your project.
        _ Mark which of their future work align with your Phase 2 ideas.
    7. Take Notes & Compare
        + For each paper, write:
        – Novel idea / contribution
        – What they do better than older systems
        – What they leave as challenge / future
        – Which parts you can adopt in your project now / later
+ What the system is, why they built it, and what it achieved.
+ “What problem do they solve? How?”

For DocHop-QA: Towards Multi-Hop Reasoning over Multimodal Document Collections :
    + Keywords:
    _ Multi-hop reasoning is a type of logical thinking used in AI and human problem-solving where multiple pieces
    of information (or "hops") are combined across different sources or steps to reach a final answer or conclusion.
    _ Unimodal involves only one type of data or one mode of information. In this case, the mode is text
    
    1. The new idea and improvement:
        _ DocHop-QA introduces a large-scale benchmark for multi-hop question answering (QA) over multimodal scientific documents (text, tables, layout).
        _ It supports open-ended reasoning across multiple documents without relying on hyperlinks or shallow reasoning paths.
    + problem:
        _ Existing QA datasets (e.g., HotpotQA, MuSiQue) are:
            > Limited to Wikipedia and plain text.
            > Focused on single-hop or short answers.
            > Lack real-world complexity, multimodal content, and layout awareness.
    +🏆 Key Contributions:       
    _ dataset of 11,379 QA instances from PubMed articles.
    _ Covers 11 scientific reasoning concepts (e.g., Problem→Solution, Mechanism→Effect).
    _ Supports multi-hop, multimodal, and layout-aware reasoning.
    _ Evaluated across 4 QA tasks: structured prediction, generative answering, multimodal integration.

    2. Architecture Diagrams
    + 📊 Modules Identified:
    _ Retriever: Uses TF-IDF + BERTScore to find relevant snippets.
    _ Generator: LLMs (e.g., Qwen2-Instruct) generate questions and answers.
    _ Fusion / Aggregation: Combines snippets from multiple documents.
    _ Layout-aware modeling: Uses LayoutLMv3, LayoutXLM for spatial understanding.

    3.3. Method / Model Sections:
    🔍 Retrieval Techniques
        _ Fan-hop: Aggregates semantically related snippets.
        _ Chain-hop: Links structured (tables) and unstructured (text) content.

    🧠 Reasoning Modules
        _ Uses semantic scaffolds (e.g., Problem→Solution→Effect).
        _ Fine-tunes LLMs with LoRA for efficient training.

    🧩 Fusion of Context
        _ Combines snippets from multiple documents using semantic similarity.
        _ Table-oriented QA uses deterministic logic + keyword filtering.

    🖼️ Visual/Layout Modeling
        _ Uses OCR and bounding boxes.
        _ Models like LayoutLMv3 and LayoutXLM for spatial reasoning.

    ❓ Explainability
        _ Evaluation includes snippet-level accuracy and completeness.
        _ Human annotators provide feedback on answer quality.

Date: 19 / 10 / 2025;
For: Enhancing Document-Level Question Answering via Multi-Hop Retrieval-Augmented Generation with LLaMA 
1. Abstract + Introduction + Contributions
🆕 Novel Idea / Improvement:
_ Introduces FinLLaMA-RAG, a multi-hop Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) system built on LLaMA 3.
_ Combines dense retrieval, context fusion, and multi-hop reasoning with a joint optimization loss to improve both retrieval and generation.
🧩 Problem Addressed:
_ Existing RAG models struggle with multi-hop reasoning and context fusion across long, structured documents.
_ Especially problematic in financial QA, where answers span multiple linked documents.


2. Architecture Diagrams
📊 Key Modules (Fig. 1 & 2):

Query Embedding Module: Embeds query using LLaMA 3.
Document Retrieval Module: Retrieves top-k chunks via cosine similarity.
Contextual Fusion Layer: Aggregates chunks using attention weights.
Multi-Hop Reasoning Module: Iteratively refines context across hops.
Generation Module: Produces final answer using LLaMA 3.
Loss Function: Combines retrieval and generation losses.

🔍 Comparison Tip:

If your system uses basic retrieval + generation, consider adding multi-hop reasoning and context fusion layers.
Their use of joint loss is a good idea to balance retrieval and generation quality.


3. Method / Model Sections
🔍 Focus Areas:

Retrieval: Dense vector embeddings + cosine similarity.
Reasoning: Multi-hop updates using LLaMA 3.
Fusion: Attention-based aggregation of document chunks.
Explainability: Embedding visualizations (PCA), re-ranking scores.
Preprocessing: Tokenization, embedding, and indexing.

❓ Mark for Review:

The multi-hop reasoning equations and loss function may need deeper study if you're unfamiliar with attention-weighted fusion or joint optimization.


4. Evaluation / Experiments
📚 Datasets Used:

FinDER, FinQABench, FinanceBench, TATQA, FinQA.

📏 Metrics Reported:

nDCG@10 (retrieval ranking)
BLEU, ROUGE-L, F1 (generation quality)

📈 Performance:

FinLLaMA-RAG outperforms BERT, FinBERT, GPT-3, and traditional RAG across all metrics.
Ablation shows that removing retrieval or generation components significantly reduces performance.


5. Ablation Studies and Error Analysis
🔍 Key Findings:

Retrieval-only and generation-only models perform worse.
Multi-hop reasoning and fusion are critical for performance.
Re-ranking improves retrieval precision.

💡 Inspiration:

Consider adding re-ranking and multi-hop fusion to your system.
Use ablation to test which modules contribute most to performance.


6. Limitations / Future Work
⚠️ Limitations:

Focused on financial QA; may need adaptation for other domains.
No mention of latency or real-time performance.

🚀 Future Work Ideas:

Apply FinLLaMA-RAG to tax strategy, fraud detection, and compliance.
Extend to interactive assistants for SMEs and individuals.

🎯 Alignment with Your Phase 2:

If your project involves document Q&A, this system’s multi-hop + fusion + joint loss approach is highly relevant.
